4. Establish which revision is authoritative, with the project author's confirmation if the document itself does not settle the question.

If authority is ambiguous, stop and request a decision. Do not choose a revision silently.

## Panel-by-panel comparison procedure

Compare all 65 panels against the established authoritative screenplay. Preserve every existing panel ID and the original panel order during review.

For each panel, record:
- Panel ID and position.
- Relevant screenplay scene, page, heading, or passage.
- Whether the panel is directly supported by the screenplay.
- Any omitted, changed, or ambiguous detail.
- Any visual implication that introduces an event or fact not established by the screenplay.
- Disposition and reviewer notes.

Use these dispositions:

- **SUPPORTED** — the panel faithfully represents material in the authoritative screenplay.
- **SUPPORTED WITH NOTE** — the panel is broadly supported but needs a documented qualification or clarification.
- **ALTERED / INCOMPLETE** — a screenplay detail is missing, changed, or represented inaccurately.
- **UNSUPPORTED ADDITION** — the panel introduces an event, action, prop, setting detail, or implication not supported by the screenplay.
- **AMBIGUOUS — AUTHOR DECISION REQUIRED** — the screenplay does not resolve the interpretation sufficiently.
- **NOT REVIEWED** — no source comparison has yet been completed.

Do not convert an ambiguous panel into a confirmed story fact. Do not add dialogue, timing, story beats, or causal explanations during verification.

## Continuity and scientific constraints

The comparison must preserve established Episode 1 distinctions:

- Khaled is Character B; Kevin is Character A.

- The Moon is a separate, unidentified speaking presence communicating through station infrastructure. Its actual origin remains unresolved.
