SERIES PREMISE
HOLOS is a reality-first science-fiction universe built around relations, information, entropy,
geometry, gravity, spacetime and emergence. The story uses scientific concepts as dramatic
material while keeping established results, active research questions, HOLOS interpretations
and speculation distinct. The lunar anomaly and all characters/events are fictional; they do
not establish that the Moon is conscious or that information directly creates gravity.



CAST
KHALED — SENIOR RELATIONAL PHYSICIST
  Brilliant, controlled, sarcastic and deeply sceptical. He would rather destroy his favourite
  hypothesis than defend a false one.
KEVIN — JUNIOR SYSTEMS INVESTIGATOR
  Intelligent, impulsive, funny, insecure and occasionally an absolute liability. He wants to
  make a discovery that changes everything, preferably before lunch.
THE MOON — THE LUNAR INTERFACE
  A disturbing voice generated by the interconnected lunar observation systems. Whether it
  represents an emergent system, a malfunctioning computational process or something
  else remains unknown.
ORBIT — STATION AI
  A brutally literal operational assistant with no apparent sense of humour.

FINAL PRODUCTION DIRECTION
HOLOS — THE FUNDAMENTAL STRUCTURE OF REALITY
Episode 1 establishes the series’ central fictional universe and its intellectual foundations.

The episode must preserve the distinction between established physical principles, active
scientific questions, HOLOS interpretations and speculative possibilities.

The lunar anomaly is fictional. It is not presented as evidence that the Moon possesses
consciousness, that information directly creates gravity, or that a universal information–
geometry invariant has been discovered.

The story’s central mystery is whether the apparent separation between physical systems
conceals a deeper relational structure.

The dramatic engine of the series is investigation: observations generate hypotheses,
hypotheses are tested, assumptions fail, and the characters must confront what the evidence
actually permits them to conclude.

THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
  “We are not trying to make our theory right. We are trying to find out what is right.”
