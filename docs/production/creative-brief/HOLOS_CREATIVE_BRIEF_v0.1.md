## 1. Logline

At a heavily shielded lunar research station, a careful relational physicist and his impulsive junior investigator discover a strange pattern across three observation regions—and encounter an unidentified voice that challenges their understanding of boundaries, while a dangerous feedback event forces them to test what they think they know before the station comes apart.

## 2. Series premise

**HOLOS** is an adult animated science-fiction mystery/comedy about two researchers investigating events at the edge of what they can explain. Each story begins with an observation, follows competing explanations and practical consequences, and asks what the evidence actually permits the characters to conclude.

The series dramatizes ideas from physics, mathematics, information, geometry, entropy, gravity, systems and emergence. Fictional events are not proof of new physical laws. The show can explore big questions without presenting metaphor, coincidence or mystery as scientific fact.

**Story engine:** Observe → question → test → fail → revise → continue.

## 3. Tone and audience

**Tone:** Intelligent, irreverent adult science-fiction mystery, mixing dark comedy, intellectual curiosity, suspense and occasional emotional weight.

Comedy comes from Khaled's dry scepticism, Kevin's ambition and impulsiveness, ORBIT's literal operational responses, and the contrast between cosmic-scale problems and ordinary human reactions. The mystery should feel strange and consequential, not randomly mystical. Scientific ideas should be understandable from the scene rather than buried in jargon. Humour must not erase danger or emotion.

**Audience:** Adults, 18+. The Episode 1 screenplay cover specifies 18+ and identifies strong language, dark humour, disturbing imagery, science-fiction violence and mature themes.

**Format:** Episodic animated science-fiction with recurring characters and a continuing larger mystery. Pilot runtime and standard episode length remain undecided.

**Quality aspiration:** Premium, cinematic adult animation with expressive acting, distinctive environments, precise editing, excellent sound and memorable writing. This is a quality benchmark, not a request to copy another series' protected style or characters.

## 4. Core cast and visual identity

### KHALED — Senior relational physicist
- **Reference-sheet facts:** 38 years old; 6'1"; brown hair; blue eyes; senior researcher.
- **Visual identity:** Mature, composed face; recognisable brown-hair silhouette; dark structured high-collar HOLOS uniform; warm gold/amber piping and insignia accent.
- **Personality/function:** Analytical, controlled, observant, sarcastic and sceptical. Will entertain radical explanations but insists on definitions, measurements, controlled tests and evidence. Would rather abandon an attractive hypothesis than defend it without support.

### KEVIN — Junior systems investigator
- **Reference-sheet facts:** 23 years old; 6'0"; red hair; blue eyes; junior investigator.
- **Age discrepancy:** One exploratory concept board says 25. The paired character sheets say 23. Use 23 as the working baseline; retain 25 as an unresolved concept-art conflict until the creator decides.

Wants to make a discovery and is more willing than Khaled to touch the machine or test an idea before understanding the consequences. Not stupid: his curiosity is valuable, but he must learn that confidence is not evidence.
- **Performance:** More kinetic than Khaled—quick glances, expressive eyebrows, reactive posture and fast hand movements. Physical comedy should come from timing and consequence, not constant exaggeration.

### ORBIT — Station AI / system voice
- **Identity:** The lunar station's AI or system voice. It is distinct from the Moon.
- **Visual rule:** Current working bible describes ORBIT as non-humanoid, represented through station displays, wall panels and consoles. The ORBIT control-room concept—with Region A/B/C panels and a shared temporal-alignment display—is the working interface reference.
- **Personality/function:** Literal, protocol-driven, precise and operational. Reports readings, status, warnings, permissions and system actions. Humour comes from clinical accuracy and literal responses.
- **Not approved:** A spherical robot companion is not the default ORBIT design. Orb-like imagery in posters remains exploratory.
- **Open:** Exact voice, vocal performer/synthesis, visual state library, capabilities and limitations.

### THE MOON — Unidentified speaking presence
- **Identity:** A separate, unidentified presence communicating through station infrastructure.
- **Rule:** Its origin, motives, capabilities and relationship to the physical Moon are not established. Do not prematurely turn it into a humanoid deity, holographic woman, AI, ORBIT, station malfunction or confirmed conscious celestial body.
- **Story function:** Challenges assumptions about boundaries, separation, systems and relationships. Its statements are part of the mystery, not automatically reliable scientific explanations.
- **Visual direction:** Begin indirectly—with altered displays, synchronized signals, unexplained transmissions, environmental changes, sound and light. Face-like or personified Moon designs remain exploratory until approved.

## 5. World rules used by the pilot

1. **Setting:** A heavily shielded lunar research station at Mare Umbrae. The pilot uses the central observation chamber, research lab, Region B lower array/corridor and central control systems.
2. **Regions:** The investigation is organized around Regions A, B and C. The screenplay presents a joint binary record in which only 000 and 111 appear. That is a fictional observation, not a universal law.
3. **Geometry/gravity:** Instruments detect a gravitational or geometric deviation correlated in time with the joint-state anomaly. Correlation does not establish causation.
4. **Competing explanations:** Known physical coupling, shared equipment, timing, environmental effects, hidden channels, measurement error and new hypotheses remain distinguishable possibilities until tested.
5. **Separate presences:** ORBIT is the station AI. The Moon is a distinct, unidentified presence. Neither voice may be substituted for the other.
6. **Interventions:** Changing partitions or feedback pathways changes the experiment and may create new behaviour. It does not prove a metaphysical claim about reality.
7. **Evidence discipline:** Distinguish direct observation, inference, hypothesis and unknown. An unexplained event is not automatic proof of new physics, consciousness, magic or an information-to-gravity law.

## 6. Episode 1 story promise

Khaled and Kevin investigate an unusual joint-state pattern across three lunar observation regions while instruments register a gravitational/geometric deviation. A strange voice begins communicating through station infrastructure. Kevin's unauthorized processor connection creates an uncontrolled feedback pathway and puts the station in danger. Khaled leads controlled tests and restores the original partition. The immediate crisis is contained, but no final explanation is established. A new Region C signal and an unidentified message open a larger mystery.

The pilot should introduce the core cast, make the A/B/C pattern understandable, reveal the speaking presence without explaining it, give Kevin's mistake real consequences, demonstrate Khaled's scientific discipline, resolve the immediate emergency and leave one compelling unanswered question. The existing screenplay's seven scenes and post-credit sequence are the working story structure; use the local DOCX as the source for exact dialogue and actions. Reconcile the GitHub text extraction before declaring it verbatim.

## 7. Visual approach

**Current direction:** Cinematic science-fiction environments; expressive stylized human character design; dark structured uniforms; metallic lunar-station interiors; deep graphite/navy and lunar blues; cyan interface lighting; warm gold accents for Khaled and red accents for Kevin.

**Continuity:** Keep Region A/B/C displays in consistent spatial positions. Interfaces must be legible at final output size and display only story-relevant information. Use cinematic depth, deliberate framing and atmospheric lighting without obscuring scientific action.

The art establishes character identity and a broad visual direction; it does not yet lock the final animation medium. Do not claim 2D, 3D or hybrid implementation as final until the actual character rigs and dependencies are recovered and tested.

## 9. Relationship to the HOLOS research programme

The animation series and scientific research programme share a name and related ideas, but are separate workstreams. The series may dramatize information, relations, entropy, geometry, gravity, spacetime and emergence. The research programme determines what is established, open, speculative or unsupported. Dialogue should not turn a HOLOS interpretation into a claim of established science.

Keep the repositories distinct:
- Production: kalmahdy326-lang/HOLOS
- Research: kalmahdy326-lang/holos-reality

Scientific claims used in an episode should receive a brief research and wording check before dialogue lock.

## Governing creative principle

**The universe can be strange. The characters can be ridiculous. The evidence cannot be treated as optional.**

HOLOS should make the audience curious about reality, attached to its characters and eager for the next mystery—without pretending that the show has solved questions it has only just learned how to ask.
