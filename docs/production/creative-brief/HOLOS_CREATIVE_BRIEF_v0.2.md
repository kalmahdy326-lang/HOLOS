## 1. Series identity

**Logline**

At a lunar research colony, two investigators uncover an impossible pattern in three observation regions and a strange signal associated with the Moon. Their investigation turns dangerous when an experiment destabilizes the station, forcing them to question what they can actually prove about the structure of reality.

**Premise**

HOLOS is a cinematic science-fiction series with comedy, mystery and adventure. It follows Khaled and Kevin as they investigate extraordinary events using observation, argument, experimentation and occasionally terrible judgement. Scientific and philosophical ideas—relationships, information, geometry, gravity, entropy, systems and emergence—provide story material, while the characters' contrasting personalities create humour and momentum.

The series can be ambitious and strange without presenting fictional events as proof of real scientific discoveries.

**Tone:** Serious, adventurous science fiction with irreverent character comedy, dry humour, suspense and moments of wonder. The universe should feel large and mysterious; the characters should feel human, fallible and funny. Comedy comes from their personalities, decisions and consequences—not from making every event a joke.

**Audience:** The Episode 1 screenplay specifies an 18+ audience and mature content, including strong language, dark humour, disturbing imagery and science-fiction violence. Maintain that rating direction unless the creator changes it.

**Format:** An animated pilot introducing a repeatable episodic series engine, recurring characters and a larger unresolved mystery. Pilot runtime and standard series episode length remain to be confirmed against the authoritative screenplay and production plan.

## 2. Core cast and non-negotiable visual identity

The opening sequence and existing character sheets are the primary visual references. Preserve recognisable designs, clothing silhouettes, colour relationships and proportions. Do not replace them with designs from speculative concept art.

### KHALED — Lead investigator / relational physicist

- **Visible identity:** Young-looking man with dark, curly hair and freckles.
- **Signature appearance:** Scarf and earth-toned clothing, matching the established Khaled reference direction.
- **Character function:** The more analytical, measured investigator. He questions assumptions, studies evidence and separates what is observed from what is merely proposed.
- **Performance:** Controlled reactions, dry sarcasm, deliberate looks and gestures, with emotion becoming more visible under pressure.
- **Continuity:** Use the creator-approved Khaled sheet and the opening as the on-screen model authority. The existing sheet lists him as 38; preserve that profile unless the creator changes it. Do not infer a different age from his youthful screen appearance.

### KEVIN — Co-investigator / systems investigator

- **Visible identity:** Red-haired male companion with a distinctive swept/spiked hairstyle.
- **Signature appearance:** Dark tactical/science-fiction clothing, retaining the silhouette and red-haired identity shown in the opening and character references.
- **Character function:** Curious, ambitious, mischievous, rebellious and more impulsive than Khaled. He is capable and intelligent, but confidence can outrun his assessment of risk.
- **Performance:** Faster physical reactions, expressive eyebrows, quick glances, restless hands and more kinetic movement than Khaled.
- **Continuity:** Use the matching Kevin reference sheet and opening model. The paired reference sheet lists him as 23; one concept board says 25. Treat 23 as the current reference-sheet baseline until the creator resolves the discrepancy.

### ORBIT — Spherical station robot / AI assistant

- **Visible identity from the opening:** A small white spherical robotic character, with glowing blue eyes and a friendly smile; the opening labels it ORBIT.
- **Story function:** ORBIT is the station's AI/assistant presence. Its relationship to station systems and interfaces should remain coherent with the screenplay.
- **Personality direction:** Helpful, precise and literal, with humour emerging from its timing, observations and responses. Final voice/performance detail remains to be approved.
- **Continuity:** The spherical robot is the established opening design cue—not an unrelated speculative companion. Station panels and interface graphics may represent ORBIT's system functions, but must not erase or contradict the visible spherical character.
- **Do not confuse ORBIT with the Moon's unidentified speaking presence.** They are separate story entities.

### THE MOON — Unresolved presence / central mystery

- **Opening visual identity:** A cracked, visibly dual-toned Moon. Preserve this strong opening image as a defining visual motif.
- **Story function:** The Moon and the unexplained signal or presence associated with it form the pilot's mystery and challenge the investigators' assumptions.
- **Continuity rule:** Do not prematurely establish the Moon as a humanoid deity, a conventional villain, ORBIT, or a proven conscious celestial body. The exact nature and origin of the presence remain unresolved unless established by the authoritative screenplay or a later creator decision.
- **Visual direction:** Use the cracked dual-tone surface, lunar scale, atmosphere, lighting and environmental effects to communicate tension and mystery. Additional face-like/personified artwork remains exploratory unless separately approved.

## 3. Pilot world and story rules

- **Setting:** A lunar colony/research station. The Episode 1 screenplay focuses on the station, its observation systems and the surrounding lunar environment.
- **Observation regions:** The investigation uses Regions A, B and C. The screenplay describes a joint binary record in which only 000 and 111 appear.
- **Anomaly:** A gravitational or geometric deviation is observed in temporal association with the unusual data pattern. This is an observation, not proof of causation.
- **Investigation:** Khaled and Kevin consider competing explanations and conduct tests. Shared equipment, timing, ordinary physical coupling, environmental factors, hidden channels and measurement error must not be dismissed without evidence.
- **Incident:** Kevin's intervention creates an uncontrolled feedback pathway and puts the station at risk. Khaled attempts controlled isolation/testing and restoration of the original arrangement.
- **Mystery:** The Moon-associated voice/presence communicates through station infrastructure. Its origin is not settled by the fact that the message arrives through the station.
- **Evidence discipline:** Keep observation, inference, hypothesis and unknown distinct. An unexplained event is not automatic proof of new physics, consciousness, magic or an information-to-gravity law.
- **Cast distinction:** ORBIT is the spherical robotic AI assistant; the Moon-associated presence is separate and unidentified.

## 4. Visual and cinematic direction

**Overall look:** Cinematic, high-production science fiction with a serious yet adventurous atmosphere. Combine expressive, recognisable character animation with expansive lunar imagery, a lived-in colony, detailed research environments and readable futuristic interfaces.

**Visual anchors**
- The cracked, dual-toned Moon as the dominant mystery image.
- The lunar colony/research station as a practical, inhabited setting rather than a generic spaceship bridge.
- Khaled's dark curls, freckles, scarf and earth-toned clothing.
- Kevin's red hair and dark tactical/science-fiction outfit.
- ORBIT's compact white spherical body and glowing blue eyes/smile.
- Strong cinematic contrast between lunar scale, station interiors and character close-ups.

**Animation priorities:** Consistent on-model characters; readable posing and eye-lines; believable weight and interaction; clear acting choices; strong camera composition; controlled effects; and facial/lip-sync performance tied to approved dialogue and audio.

The final 2D/3D/hybrid implementation must follow the recovered source rigs and tested production workflow. Do not claim a final animation medium or production-ready rigs before validation.

## 6. Production philosophy and engineering boundary

HOLOS production must remain:

- **Local-first:** core production operates on the creator's own machine.
- **Offline-capable:** rendering, assembly and validation must not require an AI service or cloud account.
- **Modular:** characters, scenes, shots, dialogue, audio and assembly remain independently manageable.
- **Reproducible:** explicit JSON configurations, manifests and versioned outputs.
- **Preservational:** source art, scripts, soundtrack, working prototypes and completed work are never silently replaced.

**Production responsibilities**

| Technology | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Godot | Character rigs, animation, scenes and rendering |
| Python | Production controller, configuration, automation, validation and reporting |
| FFmpeg | Audio/video assembly and encoding |
| FFprobe | Media inspection and technical validation |
| JSON | Episode, scene, shot, dialogue and render configuration |
| Git | Version control, history and review |

**Current engineering focus — Milestone 6: Local Production Engine**

Unify the production controller and Godot renderer; support configured individual shots and ordered episodes; maintain resumable manifests; assemble validated media with FFmpeg/FFprobe; produce reports; and preserve originals with safe backups and restoration.

Khaled's M3 and Kevin's M4 work are functional facial/lip-sync prototypes, not proof of final production-quality rigs. Recovery/integration of the original rig source scenes and dependencies remains a gate before full animation production.

External AI—including the optional Mistral chat app—may assist planning or development only. It is not a mandatory production dependency and must not become a requirement for rendering or completing an episode.
