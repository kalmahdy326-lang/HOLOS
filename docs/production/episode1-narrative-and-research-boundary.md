# HOLOS — Episode 1 Narrative Design and Scientific Research Boundary

**Project author:** Khaled AL-MAHDY  
**Episode:** 1 — *The Moon Has Anger Issues*  
**Purpose:** Keep scientific ideas understandable, character-driven and dramatically useful without turning fictional events into scientific evidence.

## 1. The two HOLOS repositories have different jobs

- **Animation/production repository:** story canon, characters, dialogue, visual direction, screenplay provenance, production assets and rendering tools.
- **Scientific research repository:** independent mathematical investigations, sources, derivations, experiments and falsification attempts.

The scientific research repository is `kalmahdy326-lang/holos-reality`. Investigations INV-001 through INV-014 belong to that research programme. The animation repository may use scientific ideas as story material, but it does not own, validate or prove the research.

The production project must not:
- copy fictional results into the research repository as empirical evidence;
- treat a character's dialogue as a scientific derivation;
- claim the episode proves a new theory of gravity, entropy, information or spacetime;
- make a fictional anomaly stand in for a reproducible experiment;
- silently rewrite research conclusions to fit a plot.

The research project must not:
- treat screenplay choices as source material for physics claims;
- allow dramatic convenience to alter a derivation or test result;
- use fictional dialogue as a citation or empirical observation.

## 2. What is established for the Episode 1 premise

The current production context describes:
- a heavily shielded lunar research station in Mare Umbrae;
- Khaled (Character B), a senior relational physicist/researcher;
- Kevin (Character A), a junior systems investigator;
- observations labelled regions A, B and C;
- binary records containing only `000` and `111`;
- an accompanying gravitational/geometric deviation;
- an unidentified speaking presence called the Moon, communicating through station infrastructure;
- ORBIT as a separate station AI/system voice, characterized as protocol-driven and literal.

The observation is not itself a causal explanation. In particular, the correlation between the binary pattern and the geometric deviation does not establish that one causes the other.

## 3. What remains unresolved

The phrase “The Moon Has Anger Issues” is the episode title and a dramatic framing. The exact physical cause, agency, origin and mechanism of the Moon's apparent anger must be taken from the authoritative screenplay and creator-approved canon.

Do not invent:
- a definitive origin for the Moon's voice;
- a humanoid Moon appearance;
- an explanation for why the Moon communicates;
- a causal mechanism linking the 000/111 records to gravity;
- an answer to whether the Moon is conscious, artificial, natural or something else;
- additional scientific rules not present in the screenplay or approved story bible.

Where the screenplay source is not available, mark the detail UNKNOWN / SOURCE REQUIRED. A synopsis is not a substitute for the screenplay.

## 4. Scientific exposition through character interaction

Use a three-layer scene design:

### Layer A — observable event
Show what the instruments, station or characters can directly observe. Use concrete nouns, readings, alarms, plots, samples and physical actions. Let the audience see the anomaly before hearing an explanation.

### Layer B — competing interpretation
Let Khaled distinguish observation from inference. Let Kevin challenge assumptions, ask practical questions and test an alternative. They should not simply take turns delivering textbook paragraphs.

### Layer C — consequence or test
Make the scientific idea matter to the next decision: what to measure, what to isolate, what prediction follows, what result would falsify the current explanation, or what risk the characters face.

A useful dialogue rhythm:
1. anomaly appears;
2. Kevin reacts or proposes a quick test;
3. Khaled clarifies what is and is not known;
4. ORBIT supplies a precise system fact or protocol constraint;
5. the test produces a result;
6. the result narrows possibilities but does not overclaim;
7. a new question or consequence advances the story.

Keep character voices distinct. Khaled should not be infallible; Kevin should not exist only to be corrected. Their disagreement should improve the investigation.

## 5. Accessibility rules for complex ideas

- Introduce one unfamiliar concept at a time.
- Anchor abstract terms in a visible measurement or ordinary analogy.
- Explain the limitation of an analogy when it matters.
- Avoid defining a technical term and immediately using several more.
- Prefer a question-and-test exchange over a lecture.
- Keep the audience aware of what the characters know, suspect and do not know.
- Never use a simplified analogy as if it were the literal physics.

For relational physics, the dramatic point can be that a measurement describes a relationship among systems, not necessarily an isolated object's intrinsic property. The exact technical wording must be checked against the relevant research and screenplay, not invented in production notes.

## 6. Five voice identities and external narrator

The available production context refers to five established voice identities and a mysterious external narrator, but the authoritative mapping, source recordings, speaker labels and approved usage rules have not been verified in the material inspected for this document.

Therefore no names, character assignments, narrator identity, voice actor credits or audio file mapping are invented here.

Before automated assembly, create a voice registry with:
- stable `voice_id`;
- canonical speaker/character ID (or `unresolved`);
- source recording paths and SHA-256;
- source owner/provenance;
- approved usage and exclusions;
- sample rate, channel count and format;
- dialogue segment IDs and alignment references;
- whether the voice is diegetic dialogue, ORBIT/system output, Moon communication, narrator or other;
- explicit creator approval status.

Keep narrator audio on its own track/segment class until its identity and story relationship are confirmed. Do not automatically assign the theme singer to the narrator or a character.

The current FFmpeg assembly layer supports optional dialogue and music inputs and can mix dialogue/music with a configurable music gain. The current v1 episode config does not define five voice identities, per-line speaker routing or phoneme alignment. That requires a future versioned audio/dialogue schema and tests.

## 7. Canonical source order and editorial protection

For plot, dialogue and scene order:
1. original screenplay DOCX, once retrieved and compared;
2. explicit creator-approved corrections;
3. canonical Series Bible;
4. source-linked storyboard and breakdown;
5. derivative summaries and production notes.

A text-only storyboard is a planning aid, not finished panels or an approved final cut. Preserve all existing storyboard IDs/order until an explicit edit is approved.

If screenplay and derivative documents disagree, log the conflict with exact source references. Do not resolve it by guessing.
