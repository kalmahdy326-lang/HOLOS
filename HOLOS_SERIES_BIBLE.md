## 1. Series identity

**Series title:** HOLOS — *The Fundamental Structure of Reality*
**Episode 1 title in the current screenplay extraction:** “The Moon Has Anger Issues”
**Format and audience:** The extracted Episode 1 title page describes HOLOS as an adult animated science-fiction series and labels the material 18+ for strong language, dark humour, disturbing imagery, science-fiction violence, and mature themes. This is the content positioning stated by that screenplay extraction; final rating and distribution classification have not been independently set here.
**Genre and tone:** Science-fiction mystery and investigation, suspense, philosophical questions, and dark humour.
**Series author/creator credit:** Khaled AL-MAHDY.

### Series premise

HOLOS follows investigators confronting fictional anomalies in a lunar research setting and asking what observations actually permit them to conclude. Questions about relationships, information, entropy, geometry, gravity, spacetime, and emergence provide dramatic material; the series is not a claim that its fictional events establish new physics.

The Episode 1 source describes the series’ central mystery as whether apparent separation between physical systems conceals a deeper relational structure. The mystery should remain open until story evidence resolves it. No season-wide resolution or episode slate has been supplied in the materials reviewed.

### Dramatic engine

The current project direction supports a repeatable investigative pattern: observe an anomaly; separate observation from interpretation; compare ordinary explanations with more speculative alternatives; design a test; preserve the raw record; and accept uncertainty or failure when evidence does not support a stronger conclusion. Character humour and conflict emerge from how Khaled’s caution and Kevin’s impulsiveness meet under pressure.

This engine is grounded in Episode 1 and the project’s stated research-first philosophy. It is not a locked formula for every future episode.

## 2. Creative and scientific guardrails

1. Distinguish what instruments observe, what characters infer, what remains hypothetical, and what is not known.
2. Correlation is not causation. A simultaneous information-pattern and geometry deviation does not prove a general physical relation.
3. The Moon is not established to be conscious. Do not give it a humanoid face or present a particular explanation of its voice as fact.
4. Do not present the fictional lunar anomaly, a finite-dimensional information result, or a HOLOS interpretation as a derivation of gravity or spacetime.
5. Preserve alternative explanations, including known physical coupling, environmental effects, timing errors, shared equipment, hidden channels, model extensions, radical alternatives, and measurement or interpretation error.
6. Preserve raw observations before playback or interpretation where the screenplay specifies it.
7. Do not add dialogue, decide unresolved variants, or convert a draft storyboard into locked timing without approval.

The project's governing posture is inquiry, not advocacy for a theory.

## 4. World, locations, and recurring visual assets

### Established Episode 1 setting

Episode 1 is set at a heavily engineered lunar research facility in **Mare Umbrae**. The visual premise is industrial and physically credible: pressure doors, radiation shielding, sensor cables, observation towers/modules, control systems, repair infrastructure, and a visible Earth horizon. The facility should not resemble a mystical temple.

The screenplay and draft storyboard identify these locations or set states:

- Lunar station exterior and approach, with Earth above the lunar horizon.
- Central observation chamber: intact, active, distorted/damaged, stabilized, and later under repair.
- Research laboratory with a wall/interface presenting four competing explanatory models.
- Region B lower-array maintenance corridor, with a ceiling crack, panel/processor connection, pressure line, and damage continuity.
- Central control system with common-power, shared-clock, communications, and feedback controls.
- Abstract unidentified region of space for the post-credit scene, with no stars or planets and a geometric relationship grid.

## 5. Episode 1 — current story source summary

**Title in extracted screenplay:** “The Moon Has Anger Issues.”
**Structure represented by the extraction:** Seven main scenes plus a post-credit scene.
**Source limitation:** The repository copy is a 25-page text extraction of a consolidated chat-derived screenplay. Its own provenance note says the original 48,709-byte DOCX is in the Project-space Library and that this extraction was not compared verbatim with the full local attachment. The extraction is detailed, but the original binary and author’s final lock/approval record were not present on the branch reviewed. See [screenplay provenance and editorial gates](docs/episode1-screenplay-provenance-and-editorial-gates.md).

### Story overview from the extracted version

At a lunar research station, Khaled and Kevin examine three observation regions whose individual marginal records appear ordinary while their joint records show only 000 and 111. ORBIT reports a gravitational/geometry deviation correlated in time with the anomaly. A speaking presence appears through station systems and refers to the researchers’ boundaries and partitions. The characters debate ordinary, extended, radical, and erroneous explanations rather than declaring a discovery.

Khaled disconnects Region C; an earlier-timestamped transmission appears despite the disconnect. In the lower array, Kevin bypasses an authorization warning and connects a processor, creating an uncontrolled feedback pathway. The station destabilizes and Kevin is injured. Khaled and Kevin perform sequential interventions, preserve raw observations, restore the original partition, and disable the feedback layer. The geometry readings move toward baseline, but the characters do not claim to have established a general information–geometry law or the identity of The Moon.

Later, a distinct fourth signal arrives from Region C. Khaled orders the raw data preserved before playback. The Unknown Voice says, “You have been looking at the Moon. You should have been looking at the space between things.” Kevin asks if it is a software bug; Khaled cannot reassure him. In the post-credit scene, the Unknown Voice speaks about boundaries, wholes, and relations as three points illuminate in an abstract grid. The source leaves the voice and phenomenon unresolved.

### Scene map

1. **Nothing Exists Alone** — introduce the station, Khaled/Kevin banter, the joint-state pattern, and the correlated geometry deviation.
2. **The Voice of a Whole** — the anomalous voice appears; Region C is disconnected; the source remains unknown.
3. **Four Explanations and One Very Bad Idea** — competing hypotheses and the earlier-timestamped Region C packet.
4. **The Boundary Problem** — Kevin bypasses a safeguard, creates a feedback pathway, and is injured.
5. **When the Whole Bites Back** — station instability escalates; characters distinguish correlation from causation.
6. **The Test** — controlled interventions; raw observations preserved; feedback disabled; anomaly subsides without a claimed theory-proof.
7. **The Thing We Didn’t Find** — measured debrief, unresolved alternatives, the fourth signal, and cliffhanger.
8. **Post-credit** — the Unknown Voice speaks in an abstract space; no explanation is supplied.

Proposed shot coverage is not an approved animatic, locked shot list, frame timing, or completed animation.

## 9. Production pipeline and standards

HOLOS is an independent, local-first production project. The production pipeline must not require Manus, an external AI service, paid generation credits, or internet connectivity at runtime. Preserve existing sources, dialogue, soundtracks, benchmarks, and project history. Inventory and back up before structural changes; never overwrite originals during restore or integration.

| Component | Intended responsibility |
|---|---|
| Godot | Character rigs, scene/animation playback, and rendering |
| Python | Production controller, configuration, scheduling, validation, backups, logging, reporting |
| FFmpeg / FFprobe | Media inspection, audio/video assembly, encoding, technical validation |
| JSON | Episode, scene, shot, dialogue, audio, character-state, and render settings |
| Git / Git LFS | Versioned source and history; LFS for larger binary artwork, audio, and video |

The repository documents a Python-controlled workflow with individual-shot rendering, ordered resumable episode rendering, verified assembly, debug/production separation, media validation, inventory/backup/restore, manifests, and logs. The Milestone 6 document reports these implementation capabilities and CI coverage, while also stating that integration against the real Khaled/Kevin rigs and a target-machine offline acceptance test remain unverified. CI or generic adapter tests do not substitute for a disconnected local acceptance test.
